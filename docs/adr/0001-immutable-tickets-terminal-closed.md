# Tickets are immutable after creation; `closed` is terminal

Support tickets are business records: once created, `title` and `description` can never be edited — clarification belongs in ticket chat (M2) — and nothing is ever hard-deleted. Lifecycle transitions are restricted to explicit server-side actions: Support Agents move `open ↔ in_progress → resolved` and may reopen `resolved → in_progress`; a Customer may close (`→ closed`) from any non-closed status on their own tickets; Support Agents and Admins may also close (abandoned/spam tickets would otherwise linger in `in_progress` forever); no one reopens `closed` — follow-ups require a new Ticket. We chose immutability and terminality to keep the audit trail clean and the permission rules trivially enforceable, at the cost of occasional duplicate tickets for recurring issues. All transition rules live in one place, a declarative table in `tickets/policy.py`, consumed by both the action endpoints and the table-driven tests.
